Design story: product design

Designing The Sanctum one room at a time

A client asked me for a personal tool for their spiritual practice, one they could share with their coven. This is what they asked for in each room and what I built.

A 7-minute read. Updated November 2026.

The brief

A client came to me wanting a personal tool for documenting their notes and their spiritual journey in witchcraft, paganism and related paths. It had to be specific enough for their own practice. They also wanted to share it with close friends in their coven.

That coven includes witches, druids and pagans of different kinds. So the tool had to work for all of them, with specific features for each important part of a practice.

I wanted one more thing from it. The Sanctum should feel like your own private estate, where you reflect, do your work and learn. It should feel like a fun library, with none of the black-and-white flatness of a pile of notes. Recording a note should feel good enough that you want to come back.

Not every room is designed yet. I have designed the Celestial Realm and the Grimoire, and even those aren’t finished, because I’m still building the website. The client uses it now.

The Celestial Realm

What they asked for

The client wanted to understand their birth chart and astrology better. They wanted to learn about the major transits coming in their life and the ones already behind them. They wanted to compare their chart with friends, family and romantic interests. And they wanted a thorough explanation of the natal chart: what a Pisces sun means, and what each house means.

They wanted more than a place to store what they learned. They wanted the tool to teach, so it could help in general and not only hold their own notes.

Then there was the question of what a placement means. When someone says your Saturn is in Aries, the client wanted a visual that shows it. Many of them practise by the moon, so the moon phase had to be there. Their astrology is Hellenistic and modern. It covers the big planets, and it also covers points such as Black Moon Lilith and the North Node, and asteroids.

What I built

The observatory shows one moment three ways at once: a list of every planet, a bronze orrery of the solar system, and a chart wheel. Select something in one and the other two follow. Move the date and all three move together.

I kept the three views because each answers a different question. The list tells you where things are. The orrery shows why they appear there. The wheel shows what that means against your own chart. It has two rings: the inside is the birth chart and stays still, and the outside is the sky on the date you chose.

The client also wanted to see what the sky looks like from Earth when the planets sit in different places at different times. They wanted to see the planets as real bodies that move through the solar system, and to feel a connection between what they read and what they saw. For that I made the Constellation Dome, which is in preview.

The Grimoire

What they asked for

The client called the Grimoire their main place for notes on the divinations they do. Most of them read tarot, so tarot came first.

What I built

Decks and cards. You log your own decks and your own cards. The Grimoire gives you statistics on the cards you draw, so you can see whether certain ones keep coming up and spot synchronicities.

Readings. Each reading has a time, a date, a description and the details of what was happening before you drew. You can customise a lot of it, so the log works as a visual record of every part of the reading. As you understand the cards better, you can reread an old reading and see what it was trying to tell you the first time.

Dreams. The client wanted to see whether synchronicities show up between their dreams and their real life, so there is a dream journal.

Heavens. This tab holds experiences that match a transit happening at the time. If Saturn opposes your midheaven, which can mean testing and responsibility, you have a place to log what happened to you during it and make sense of it.

Journal and signs. The journal takes any thought or idea. The signs tab tracks angel numbers and when they appeared.

Spell work. You record the spells you cast, what happened and the materials you used. It works as a digital grimoire that you can run data analysis on, and it stays easy to use.

The Grimoire itself began as a list of entries. It is now a book lying open on a desk: a timeline on the left page, the entry on the right, and tabs for each subject along the side. The book has to be painted straight on, because a page that turns needs flat geometry. The first, angled painting had to be redone.

The other rooms

The client asked for four more rooms. I haven’t fully designed them yet, so here is what they asked for.

Tradition. Practitioners reflect on the wheel of the year, the sacred calendar of different cultures. Each person follows a different lens. Some worship Hellenistic gods, some Norse, some Kemetic. They wanted to know the festivals of their own pantheon in ancient times, and a way to reflect on the wheel of the year.

Temple. This room is for people who work with several gods. It holds their altars, when each was last cleaned, what sits on them and what it symbolizes, and the experiences and conversations they have had with each god.

Workshop. This is where spells get prepared. It covers spell types, a planner that finds the best time to cast according to occult sources, and a shopping list for what you need to buy. Some practitioners wanted to make sigils, so it includes the history of sigils and a space to make them digitally, hang them up and try different versions.

Archive. The client wanted somewhere to record what they already know and learn what they don’t yet: numerology, what each tarot card means, and the correspondences for each crystal, flower, herb, incense and color, with the intention behind each, linked to spells. Because some of them practise Hellenistic polytheism, it also holds Hellenistic astrology, and notes on how tarot cards relate to parts of the natal chart.
