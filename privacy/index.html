Privacy

Short, plain, honest. Updated 2026-05-22.

Who runs this site

AeroLex (operated by Noah Zepter, United States) is a one-person operation. I built the site, I read the analytics, I read your email if you send one. There is no team behind a curtain.

What data this site collects

One thing: Google Analytics 4 (GA4). When you visit a page, GA4 records a page view and some technical context. That's it. The site has no login, no payment flow, no form. If you reach out, you do that by clicking a Calendly link or an email link, and at that point you are talking to me directly on those platforms, which have their own policies.

What GA4 captures

  • Which page you visited and the URL you came from
  • Approximate location (country and region, not your address)
  • Device type, browser, screen size, language setting
  • Whether you clicked one of two specific buttons: "Book a call" or the email link
  • An anonymized, truncated version of your IP address

GA4 does not capture your name, your email address, your typing, or anything you do off this site. The site does not run ad pixels, retargeting tags, session recorders, or any of that. If a future version of the site adds anything like that, this page will say so before it goes live.

Cookies, and how to decline them

The first time you visit, you'll see a bar at the bottom of the page asking whether to accept or decline analytics cookies. Either choice is fine; the site works the same either way.

If you accept, GA4 sets two cookies (typically named _ga and _ga_KKEJ0GQJ9Z) that let it tell repeat visits apart from new ones. Your choice is stored in your browser's local storage for 365 days. If you decline, GA4 still pings a single anonymous event so I can see aggregate page-view counts, but no cookies are set and nothing about your session persists.

To change your mind later, click Cookie settings in the footer of any page, or clear your browser data for aerolex.dev.

Where the data goes

Google Analytics is operated by Google LLC and runs on Google's servers. Their privacy practices are published at policies.google.com/privacy. I do not share GA4 data with anyone else. I do not sell it. I do not feed it to advertisers. It exists so I can learn whether a LinkedIn post or a cold email actually brought anyone to the site.

How long the data sticks around

GA4 holds event-level data for up to 14 months, then deletes it. Aggregate counts (like "how many people visited /command-center/ this month") stay longer, but they're aggregate, not tied to you.

Your rights

If you're in the EU, the UK, California, or anywhere else with a meaningful privacy law: you can ask me what data is associated with you, you can ask me to delete it, and you can ask me to stop processing it. Email noah@aerolex.dev. Because the only identifier GA4 holds is a randomized cookie ID, the practical answer in most cases is "decline the cookies, or clear your browser data, and you're done." If you want me to also delete any record of your visit, say so and I'll do it.

Links to other sites

The site links to Calendly (booking), an email address (opens your mail app), LinkedIn, Substack, and a few past employers. Once you click out, you're on their site under their rules. I have no visibility into what happens after the click, beyond GA4 recording that the click happened on my page.

Changes to this page

If the data collection changes, the "Updated" date at the top of this page changes. If the change is material (a new tracker, a new third party), I'll put a banner on the home page for a couple of weeks.
