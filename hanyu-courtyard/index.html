Work / Hanyu Courtyard

Mandarin-learning adventure · In design and private playtesting

Hanyu Courtyard

You inherit a neglected estate in the mountains and promise to bring it back. Every errand on the way takes a little more Mandarin than the last.

I wanted to understand Chinese characters, why each one looks the way it does, instead of memorising them as small pictures. I couldn’t find a course that let me do that inside a story I wanted to keep playing. So I’m designing one.

It’s in private playtesting, which is why this page shows the design and not the game itself.

01 / A guided look

How a first session plays

Five moments from the opening, in order. Each one has a small design choice hiding in it, so I’ve pointed at the thing to notice.

  1. Arrive on the road

    You start outside the estate wall at dusk. The scene is one long painting you slide across, and your character walks to wherever you click.

    Notice: there’s no map and no menu to learn first. The first thing you do is walk toward a gate because you want to see what’s behind it.

  2. Meet Zhou

    Zhou Ming, the groundskeeper, is mending a table in the courtyard. He greets you in Mandarin before anyone has taught you a word of it.

    Notice: the story asks for the word first, and the lesson answers it. You learn 你好 because someone just said it to you.

  3. Take a character apart

    你, “you”, arrives in two coloured pieces: a part that marks words about people, and an old word for “you”.

    Notice: the meaning comes from the parts. That gives you something to reason from the next time you meet a character with the same piece in it.

  4. Pick its tone

    Mandarin has four tones, and each one gets a colour and a line drawn in the shape your voice makes. You choose the one you think you heard.

    Notice: tone three is green and it dips. From here on, that character is painted green everywhere in the game, so the tone travels with it.

  5. Watch it written, then paint it

    The strokes play in order once. Then you paint the character yourself on rice paper, over a faint outline at first and later from memory.

    Notice: the game checks the order and direction of every stroke and forgives a wobbly hand. A wrong stroke fades and you try again. Nothing restarts.

03 / Where it really stands

What’s built, and what’s planned

Built

Unit one, start to finish

  • The road, the gate and the front courtyard as painted scenes you walk through.
  • The first conversation with Zhou, and fifteen characters taught one at a time.
  • Tone colours, graded brush writing, and typing a word by its sound.
  • A journal that replays finished scenes, plus flashcard and grammar pages.

I’ve played it through three times and rebuilt parts of it after each one.

Planned

The estate that review rebuilds

  • Four more units: the market, making tea, the ancestral hall and the altar.
  • Daily review that restores the estate room by room. This is the idea the whole game is built around, and it isn’t playable yet.
  • Recorded audio, and a native speaker’s review of every Chinese line. Today’s lines are my drafts.
  • Playtests with learners other than me.
